A world record for every year since 1955

In 1955, Sir Hugh Beaver — then running the Guinness brewery — lost a pub argument about Europe's fastest game bird and decided somebody should write the answers down. Norris and Ross McWhirter did, and The Guinness Book of Records has been settling arguments ever since.

Guinness doesn't crown an annual champion — it's a book of records, not a contest. So this is the next best thing: one landmark record for every year from 1955 to today, and a line or two on what the person actually did.

2020s

Robots, rockets and a two-hour wall that finally fell
2026

First sub-two-hour marathon in an actual race

Sabastian Sawe (Kenya) ran 1:59:30 at the London Marathon on 26 April 2026 — the first person to break two hours in open competition, not a staged attempt. It took the marathon world record down by more than a minute.

Athletics
2025

Longest voluntary breath hold underwater

Vitomir Maričić (Croatia) held his breath for 29 minutes 3 seconds in June 2025, beating a record most freedivers assumed was close to the ceiling of human physiology.

Human limits
2024

First commercial spacewalk

Jared Isaacman and Sarah Gillis opened the hatch of SpaceX's Polaris Dawn capsule in September 2024. Every previous spacewalk in history had been made by a government astronaut.

Space
2023

Fastest marathon ever run (at the time)

Kelvin Kiptum (Kenya) ran 2:00:35 in Chicago in October 2023, aged 23. He was killed in a car crash four months later; his record stood until 2026.

Athletics
2022

First deliberate deflection of an asteroid

NASA's DART spacecraft flew into the asteroid moonlet Dimorphos at 22,000 km/h and measurably shortened its orbit — the first proof humanity can move a rock that's coming at us.

Space
2021

First powered flight on another planet

NASA's Ingenuity helicopter lifted three metres off the Martian surface on 19 April 2021 and hovered for 39 seconds. It was supposed to fly five times; it managed 72.

Space
2020

First crewed orbital launch by a private company

Bob Behnken and Doug Hurley rode a SpaceX Crew Dragon to the International Space Station in May 2020 — the first time a commercial firm, not a nation, put people in orbit.

Space

2010s

The decade humans jumped from the stratosphere
2019

First marathon distance covered in under two hours

Eliud Kipchoge (Kenya) ran 1:59:40 in Vienna in October 2019. Rotating pacemakers meant it could never be ratified — but the barrier was gone, and he knew it before the finish line.

Athletics
2018

Fastest human-made object ever built

NASA's Parker Solar Probe launched in August 2018 to fly through the Sun's outer atmosphere. It keeps breaking its own record on every pass, and has now exceeded 690,000 km/h.

Space
2017

First rope-free climb of El Capitan

Alex Honnold free soloed the 900-metre Freerider route in Yosemite in 3 hours 56 minutes, with no rope, no harness and no second chance. It's widely called the greatest solo climb ever made.

Adventure
2016

First round-the-world flight by a solar-powered aircraft

Bertrand Piccard and André Borschberg flew Solar Impulse 2 roughly 42,000 km across the planet without burning a drop of fuel. One leg over the Pacific lasted five days non-stop.

Aviation
2015

Most distant world explored close up

NASA's New Horizons probe swept past Pluto in July 2015 after nine years and five billion kilometres, turning a blurry dot into a world with ice mountains and a nitrogen glacier.

Space
2014

First soft landing on a comet

ESA's Philae lander touched down on Comet 67P in November 2014, after the Rosetta orbiter spent ten years chasing a four-kilometre lump of ice around the Sun.

Space
2013

First swim from Cuba to Florida without a shark cage

Diana Nyad, aged 64, swam 177 km in just under 53 hours on her fifth attempt, 35 years after her first. Box jellyfish stings had ended two of the earlier tries.

Endurance
2012

First person to break the sound barrier in freefall

Felix Baumgartner stepped off a balloon capsule at 38,969 m and hit 1,357 km/h on the way down — faster than sound, wearing nothing but a pressure suit.

Human limits
2011

First Rubik's Cube solve under six seconds

Feliks Zemdegs (Australia) solved a scrambled 3×3 cube in 5.66 seconds at the Melbourne Winter Open — quicker than most people can find the right colour to start on.

Speed
2010

Tallest building in the world

Dubai's Burj Khalifa opened in January 2010 at 828 m and 163 floors. More than fifteen years later, nothing built anywhere has topped it.

Engineering

2000s

Tourists in orbit, and the fastest man who ever lived
2009

Fastest 100 m and 200 m ever run

Usain Bolt (Jamaica) ran 9.58 and 19.19 at the Berlin World Championships within four days. Both records still stand, and nobody has come close to either.

Athletics
2008

Most gold medals at a single Olympics

Michael Phelps won eight swimming golds in Beijing, beating a mark that had stood for 36 years — and taking one of them by a hundredth of a second.

Olympics
2007

Fastest marathon

Haile Gebrselassie (Ethiopia) ran 2:04:26 in Berlin, the first of the two world records he set on that course. He'd started out running 10 km to school and back with his books under one arm.

Athletics
2006

Most hot dogs eaten in twelve minutes

Takeru Kobayashi (Japan) ate 53¾ hot dogs and buns at Nathan's Famous on Coney Island, taking his sixth straight title and roughly doubling what the record had been before he showed up.

Competitive eating
2005

Most distant landing on another world

ESA's Huygens probe parachuted onto Titan, Saturn's largest moon, in January 2005 — 1.2 billion km from Earth — and sent back photographs of riverbeds carved by liquid methane.

Space
2004

First privately funded crewed spaceflight

SpaceShipOne, funded by Paul Allen and flown by Mike Melvill and Brian Binnie, reached space twice in two weeks to win the $10 m Ansari X Prize — and started the private space industry.

Space
2003

Third nation to launch a human into space

Yang Liwei orbited Earth fourteen times aboard Shenzhou 5 in October 2003, making China the third country — after the USSR and the USA — to do it on its own.

Space
2002

First solo balloon flight around the world

Steve Fossett (USA) circled the globe alone in just under 14 days, on his sixth attempt. The five failures included a 9,000 m fall into the Coral Sea.

Adventure
2001

First space tourist

Dennis Tito (USA) paid a reported $20 million to spend eight days aboard the International Space Station, over the loud objections of NASA.

Space
2000

Longest continuous human presence in space

The Expedition 1 crew — Bill Shepherd, Yuri Gidzenko and Sergei Krikalev — moved into the ISS on 2 November 2000. There has been at least one human off the planet every day since.

Space

1990s

Supersonic on land, and the first page on the web
1999

First non-stop balloon flight around the world

Bertrand Piccard and Brian Jones flew Breitling Orbiter 3 from Switzerland to Egypt the long way round — 19 days, 21 hours, and about 40,000 km without touching down.

Adventure
1998

First film to gross a billion dollars

Titanic passed $1 billion worldwide in March 1998. It was the most expensive film ever made and was widely expected to sink the studio instead.

Film
1997

First supersonic land speed record

Andy Green (UK) drove ThrustSSC to 1,227.985 km/h across Nevada's Black Rock Desert, becoming the first person to break the sound barrier on the ground. It still stands.

Speed
1996

First computer to beat a reigning world chess champion

IBM's Deep Blue took a game off Garry Kasparov under tournament conditions in February 1996. Kasparov won the match — but lost the rematch a year later.

Technology
1995

Longest triple jump

Jonathan Edwards (UK) jumped 18.29 m in Gothenburg — twice in one afternoon, breaking his own world record on both attempts. Thirty years on, no one has matched it.

Athletics
1994

Longest undersea tunnel

The Channel Tunnel opened in May 1994 with a 37.9 km stretch running beneath the seabed — still the longest undersea section anywhere, six years and 13,000 workers in the making.

Engineering
1993

Highest high jump

Javier Sotomayor (Cuba) cleared 2.45 m in Salamanca — about 30 cm above his own head. The bar hasn't moved since.

Athletics
1992

Fastest production car

The Jaguar XJ220 hit 349 km/h (217 mph) at Nardò in Italy, taking the title off Ferrari's F40 and holding it for the rest of the decade.

Speed
1991

Longest long jump

Mike Powell (USA) jumped 8.95 m in Tokyo, finally beating a record that had stood untouched for 23 years — in a head-to-head duel with Carl Lewis that both men called the best of their lives.

Athletics
1990

The first website

Tim Berners-Lee built the first web server and web page at CERN in December 1990, on a NeXT machine with a note taped to it reading "do not power down". Everything online descends from that page.

Technology

1980s

Everest without oxygen, and a man flying free of his ship
1989

Only spacecraft to visit all four giant planets

Voyager 2 swept past Neptune in August 1989, completing a Jupiter–Saturn–Uranus–Neptune grand tour made possible by a planetary alignment that recurs roughly every 175 years.

Space
1988

Fastest women's 100 m and 200 m

Florence Griffith-Joyner (USA) ran 10.49 and 21.34 in a single summer. Nearly four decades later, both records are still standing.

Athletics
1987

First swim across the Bering Strait

Lynne Cox (USA) swam 4.3 km through 4°C water from Alaska's Little Diomede to Soviet Big Diomede. Reagan and Gorbachev both toasted the swim at the INF Treaty signing.

Endurance
1986

First non-stop unrefuelled flight around the world

Dick Rutan and Jeana Yeager flew the aircraft Voyager for 9 days without landing, taking turns lying down in a cabin the size of a phone box.

Aviation
1985

Largest live television audience of its era

Live Aid was broadcast from London and Philadelphia to an estimated 1.9 billion people in 150 countries in July 1985, and raised well over £100 million for famine relief.

Broadcast
1984

First untethered spacewalk

Bruce McCandless II flew a nitrogen-jet backpack 98 m away from Space Shuttle Challenger in February 1984 — the first human being to orbit Earth as a satellite of his own.

Space
1983

Best-selling album of all time

Michael Jackson's Thriller ran away with 1983, spending 37 weeks at number one in the US. At an estimated 70 million copies, it has never lost the Guinness title.

Music
1982

First circumnavigation of the globe via both poles

Ranulph Fiennes and Charles Burton finished the three-year Transglobe Expedition in August 1982, having crossed both poles travelling only over the surface of the Earth.

Adventure
1981

First reusable spacecraft

Space Shuttle Columbia launched in April 1981 and landed on a runway two days later, ready to fly again. It went on to make 27 more flights.

Space
1980

First solo ascent of Everest without supplemental oxygen

Reinhold Messner (Italy) climbed the mountain alone during the monsoon, with no bottled oxygen, no rope team and no fixed camps. He later said he'd felt like "a single narrow gasping lung".

Mountaineering

1970s

Pedal-powered across the Channel, and a puzzle from Budapest
1979

First human-powered flight across the English Channel

Bryan Allen pedalled the Gossamer Albatross 35 km from England to France in 2 hours 49 minutes, flying a few feet above the water the whole way.

Aviation
1978

First solo journey to the North Pole

Naomi Uemura (Japan) travelled 725 km alone by dog sled across the shifting Arctic ice, fending off a polar bear that ate most of his food along the way.

Adventure
1977

Most distant human-made object

Voyager 1 launched in September 1977 carrying a golden record of Earth's sounds. It is now more than 25 billion km away, in interstellar space, and still calling home.

Space
1976

First perfect 10 in Olympic gymnastics

Nadia Comăneci (Romania), aged 14, scored a flawless 10.0 on the uneven bars in Montreal. The scoreboard hadn't been built to show it and flashed up 1.00 instead. She scored six more.

Olympics
1975

Highest-grossing film of its time

Steven Spielberg's Jaws took over $470 million on a broken mechanical shark and a two-note theme — and invented the idea of the summer blockbuster in the process.

Film
1974

Best-selling puzzle toy ever made

Ernő Rubik, an architecture lecturer in Budapest, built the first Cube as a teaching aid — then took a month to solve it himself. Over 350 million have sold since.

Toys
1973

Fastest mile and a half on dirt

Secretariat won the Belmont Stakes by 31 lengths in 2:24 — a margin and a time no thoroughbred has come near in the half-century since.

Racing
1972

Most golds at one Olympics (at the time)

Mark Spitz (USA) won seven swimming titles in Munich and set a world record in every single one of them. The mark stood for 36 years.

Olympics
1971

First spacecraft to orbit another planet

Mariner 9 reached Mars in November 1971, waited out a planet-wide dust storm, then mapped 85% of the surface and found the largest volcano in the solar system.

Space
1970

Farthest any human has been from Earth

The crippled Apollo 13 crew swung 400,171 km out around the far side of the Moon to slingshot home. Nobody has ever been further away, and it wasn't on purpose.

Space

1960s

The deepest dive, the first spacewalk, the Moon
1969

First humans on the Moon

Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on the lunar surface on 20 July 1969. Two months earlier, the Apollo 10 crew hit 39,937 km/h coming home — still the fastest humans have ever travelled.

Space
1968

Longest long jump of its era

Bob Beamon (USA) jumped 8.90 m in Mexico City, beating the world record by 55 cm in one go. The officials' measuring device didn't reach far enough. It's still the Olympic record.

Athletics
1967

Fastest solo circumnavigation by sail

Sir Francis Chichester, aged 65, sailed around the world single-handed with one stop in Australia, in 226 days. He was knighted with the sword used on Sir Francis Drake.

Sailing
1966

First spacecraft to reach another planet

The Soviet Venera 3 probe struck Venus in March 1966 — the first human-made object to touch the surface of another planet, though its instruments had failed on the way.

Space
1965

First spacewalk

Alexei Leonov (USSR) spent 12 minutes outside Voskhod 2 in March 1965. His suit ballooned in the vacuum and he had to bleed off air to squeeze back through the hatch.

Space
1964

Only person to set land and water speed records in the same year

Donald Campbell (UK) managed 648 km/h on Australia's Lake Eyre salt flats in July and 444 km/h on water in December — a double nobody has repeated.

Speed
1963

First woman in space

Valentina Tereshkova (USSR), a 26-year-old textile worker and amateur parachutist, orbited Earth 48 times alone in Vostok 6. She is still the only woman to have flown a solo space mission.

Space
1962

Most points in an NBA game

Wilt Chamberlain scored 100 against the New York Knicks in Hershey, Pennsylvania. There's no film of it, and in 60-odd years nobody has come within 19 points.

Basketball
1961

First human in space

Yuri Gagarin (USSR) orbited the Earth once in 108 minutes on 12 April 1961, then ejected and parachuted into a field, where a farmer and her daughter found him.

Space
1960

Deepest ocean descent

Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh took the bathyscaphe Trieste 10,916 m down into the Mariana Trench. A window cracked on the way; they carried on anyway.

1950s

Where all of this started — a pub argument in Ireland
1959

First human-made object to reach another world

The Soviet Luna 2 probe crashed into the Moon in September 1959, scattering metal pennants stamped with the Soviet coat of arms across the surface.

Space
1958

Youngest World Cup winner

Pelé won the final for Brazil aged 17 years 249 days, scoring twice against Sweden and crying on the pitch afterwards. He'd been a squad afterthought at the start of the tournament.

Football
1957

First artificial satellite

The USSR launched Sputnik 1 on 4 October 1957. It was a 58 cm metal sphere that did nothing but beep — and amateur radio operators all over the world could hear it going over.

Space
1956

Only heavyweight champion to retire undefeated

Rocky Marciano walked away in April 1956 at 49 fights, 49 wins, 43 of them knockouts. No heavyweight champion has managed to leave with a clean record since.

Boxing
1955

The book that started it — best-selling copyrighted book of all time

Norris and Ross McWhirter published the first Guinness Book of Records on 27 August 1955, commissioned by a brewery managing director who'd lost an argument about game birds. It was Britain's number-one bestseller by Christmas and has since sold over 150 million copies.

A note on how this list works

Guinness World Records has never had annual winners — it isn't a competition, it's a reference book, and thousands of records are set and broken every year. So rather than invent a champion for each year, this page picks one landmark achievement per year: the record from that year that mattered most, or lasted longest, or is simply the best story.

Not every entry is an officially certified Guinness title, and some of these records have since been broken — where that's the case, it says so. Several of them, though, have gone thirty years or more without anybody getting close.
